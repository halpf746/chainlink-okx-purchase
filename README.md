## How to buy Chainlink on OKX, step by step

OKX's own guide for US customers lays the process out in five steps. What each one involves:

1. **Create the account.** Email signup on the website or in the app. Any sign-up promotion running in your country, and the terms attached to it, appears in the registration flow rather than in advance.
2. **Verify your identity.** OKX lists document-based verification as a required step under US regulations, which means KYC and anti-money-laundering checks before you can trade. Have a government ID ready, and keep the name on it identical to the name on your bank account; payment providers match the two.
3. **Fund the account.** Bank transfer or credit/debit card, plus local payment methods where OKX supports them in your country. Card money usually lands in minutes, bank transfers take longer.
4. **Find Chainlink.** Search the asset by name and select LINK.
5. **Place the order.** Enter the amount, read the quote and the fee line, then confirm. A market order fills at the taker rate. A limit order that rests in the book pays the maker rate instead.
6. **Check the balance, and the network if you plan to move it.** LINK is an ERC-20 token on Ethereum and also circulates on other chains. The withdrawal network you pick has to match the network your receiving wallet expects, or the coins won't arrive.

## How small an order can be, and why the quote moves

LINK is divisible, so nothing stops a $20 purchase. Coinbase sets its minimum at 1 unit of your local currency; Binance allows fractional orders subject to a minimum order amount for the currency and payment method you choose. Starting small is a sensible way to test the entire path, withdrawal included, before committing more.

The quote is where first-time buyers get caught out. The number on the confirmation screen is a quote, not a promise. Binance gives you one minute to confirm a card order at the quoted price and recalculates against the market if you're slower, with a refresh button for a new quote. MetaMask asks you to pull quotes from several providers and complete the transaction on the provider's page, so the figure you approve belongs to the provider rather than to MetaMask. On OKX, a Convert quote comes from OKX rather than the order book, which is why the fee can read zero while the rate is slightly worse than spot.

None of that is a hidden charge. It's the price of buying at a moment rather than at a limit.

## Where to keep the LINK after you buy

Two options, with different responsibilities attached.

Leaving LINK on OKX is custodial storage: OKX holds the keys, you log in with a password and two-factor authentication, and you can sell or withdraw as long as the account is in good standing. It's the simpler choice for a small position you may sell within a year.

Withdrawing to a wallet you control moves the keys to you. OKX frames the choice as custodial versus non-custodial storage, with hot wallets online and cold wallets offline. Once LINK leaves the exchange, no support desk can reverse a mistake. The address and the network both have to be right, and the recovery phrase needs to be backed up somewhere other than a screenshot in your camera roll.

A middle path costs almost nothing: withdraw a small test amount first, confirm it lands, then move the rest.

## How OKX compares with the other places people look at

Binance is the broadest of the group on paper: 600+ listed assets, card and bank rails in supported regions, a one-minute price lock on card orders, and staking through Binance Earn if you want yield on the LINK you're holding.

Coinbase is the most restrictive on payment methods for LINK, with no credit cards, PayPal buying limited to US customers, and gift cards US-only and excluded in Hawaii. Its trade preview screen, shown before you submit, does spell out the fee clearly.

Gemini runs a six-step flow with recurring buys on a daily, weekly, twice-monthly or monthly schedule, and states ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type 2 certification. That combination suits someone who wants an audited custodian and a fixed monthly purchase rather than a one-off trade.

MetaMask isn't an exchange account at all. LINK lands in a wallet you control, quotes come from multiple providers, and availability varies by region.

For a one-off LINK purchase funded by card or bank transfer, OKX and Binance are the closest substitutes. If holding your own keys from the first minute is the requirement, MetaMask is the direct route and the exchange account disappears from the process entirely.

## FAQ

**Can I buy less than one LINK?** Yes. LINK splits into smaller units, and fractional orders are standard on every platform above. The minimum is set per platform and per payment method, not by the token.

**Do I need to verify my identity first?** On OKX, Binance, Coinbase and Gemini, yes. Verification comes before trading, not after. MetaMask passes the fiat leg to a payment provider, which runs its own checks.

**Why did my total change between the quote and the confirmation?** Because the quote expired. Binance locks a card order for one minute and reprices after that; MetaMask hands you to a provider whose quote is the one that executes. Move quickly, or use a limit order where the venue supports one.

**Can I move LINK off OKX to my own wallet?** Yes, subject to your account status. The withdrawal screen shows the network fee before you confirm, and you're responsible for matching the network on both ends.

**Is buying LINK taxable?** In the US, gains when you sell may be subject to capital gains tax, and rewards earned through staking may count as income. OKX's guide says as much and points readers to a tax professional for their own situation.
